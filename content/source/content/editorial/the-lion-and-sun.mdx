The lion-and-sun motif combines a lion with the sun, and was a central element of Iran's national flag until 1979 [@wp-lionsun]. It appears widely on coins, metalwork, tiles and miniature paintings across Iran, Central Asia and India [@wp-lionsun].

## Where it comes from

Writers connect the motif to the astrological association of the sun with the sign of Leo, an idea some trace to ancient West Asian traditions linking sun gods and lions [@wp-lionsun]. A version with the sun half-hidden behind a lion appears in a fourteenth-century manuscript of Albumasar's *Book of Nativities*, illustrated by Qanbar ʿAli Shirazi [@wp-lionsun]. Evidence for earlier forms is debated, and the motif became widely popular from the twelfth century [@wp-lionsun]. [VERIFY with a tier A source before publication.]

## A shared symbol

The scholar Afsaneh Najmabadi has argued that the emblem has had "a unique success" as a sign of modern Iranian identity because it draws on several traditions, among them Zoroastrian, Shia and Jewish symbolism [@wp-lionsun; @najmabadi2005]. ## Use today

The emblem remains in use in different contexts. Because it is politically contested, this archive describes designs and sources and does not endorse any current use of the symbol.
