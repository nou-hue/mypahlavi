## Strain, 1976–77

By late 1976 and early 1977 the economy was in trouble, as an unrealistically ambitious industrial and military programme financed by post-1973 oil revenue strained the country's human and institutional resources; the Library of Congress also cites corruption, inflation and a growing income gap [@loc21]. Efforts to curb prices, including a war on profiteers, antagonised the bazaar and business classes [@loc21]. By 1978 there were 60,000 foreigners in Iran, 45,000 of them Americans [@loc21]. President Carter, who took office in January 1977, raised human-rights questions; the shah released political prisoners, and in July replaced Hoveyda with Jamshid Amuzegar [@loc21].

## 1978: protests

In January 1978 a government-inspired article in the newspaper Ettelaat cast doubt on Khomeini's piety; seminary students protested in Qom and several demonstrators were killed, and the protests spread, turning violent in Tabriz on 18 February [@loc21]. The protests of 1978, unlike the open letters of 1977, were led by religious elements and centred on mosques and religious events [@loc21]. More than 400 people died in a fire at the Rex Cinema in Abadan in August; the Library of Congress notes that evidence available after the revolution suggested the fire was deliberately started by religiously inclined students, while the opposition cultivated a conviction that SAVAK was responsible [@loc21].

## September to December

Martial law was declared in Tehran and eleven other cities on 7–8 September 1978; the next day troops fired into a crowd at Jaleh Square, killing, in the Library of Congress's words, "certainly many more" than the official figure of eighty-seven [@loc21]. Strikes spread through the public sector from September and, in November, shut down most private industry [@loc21]. On 5 November the shah replaced Sharif-Emami with General Gholam-Reza Azhari; marches in Tehran on 9–10 December were the largest of the year [@loc21]. Khomeini, expelled from Iraq in October, had set up headquarters at Neauphle-le-Château, near Paris [@loc21].

## January to February 1979

Shapour Bakhtiar agreed at the end of December to form a government on condition that the shah leave; he won a vote of confidence on 3 January 1979, and the shah left Iran on 16 January, announcing a "short holiday" [@loc21]. Khomeini arrived in Tehran on 1 February [@loc22]. On 11 February twenty-two senior military commanders announced that the armed forces would be neutral, and by the late afternoon of 12 February Bakhtiar was in hiding and the Pahlavi monarchy had collapsed [@loc22].

## Caution

Casualty figures, responsibility for individual events and the balance of causes remain matters of historical dispute. This archive gives the official figure alongside the Library of Congress qualification and does not offer a single total.
