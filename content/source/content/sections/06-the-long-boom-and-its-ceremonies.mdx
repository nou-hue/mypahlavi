## Growth and control

After Prime Minister Mansur was assassinated on 21 January 1965, Amir Abbas Hoveyda took office and served for twelve years, the longest term of any modern Iranian prime minister [@loc19]. The Library of Congress describes a period of impressive economic growth and relative political stability, helped by the land distribution programme, expanding jobs and moderate inflation between 1964 and 1973 [@loc19]. It also records that elections in 1967 and 1971 were carefully controlled and open only to the Mardom Party and, later, the Pan-Iranist Party, and that only 10 per cent of eligible voters in Tehran voted in the 1968 municipal elections [@loc19].

## Succession and coronation

A constituent assembly in September 1967 provided for the queen to act as regent if the crown prince had not come of age [@loc19]. In October 1967 the shah celebrated his long-postponed coronation and, like his father, placed the crown on his own head; the Majlis conferred the title Arya-Mehr, "Light of the Aryans" [@loc19]. The coronation took place on 26 October 1967 [@wp-mrp]. The Library of Congress adds that this glorification of the monarchy was not universally popular [@loc19].

## Persepolis, 1971

Celebrations in 1971 marked what was presented as 2,500 years of uninterrupted monarchy and the twenty-fifth centennial of Cyrus the Great's founding of the empire [@loc19]. They opened on 12 October 1971 at the tomb of Cyrus at Pasargadae and centred on Persepolis [@wp-2500]. The Library of Congress notes that the ceremonies drew adverse domestic comment, that honoured guests were foreign heads of state, and that Khomeini issued a declaration condemning the celebrations [@loc19]. UNESCO inscribed Persepolis on the World Heritage List in 1979 [@unesco114]. See the editorial piece on the 1971 celebrations.

## Institutions and objects

The Azadi Tower, called the Shahyad Tower until 1979, was designed by Hossein Amanat and completed in 1971 [@wp-azadi]. The Shiraz Arts Festival was held from 1967 to 1977 on the initiative of Farah Pahlavi [@wp-shiraz]. The Tehran Museum of Contemporary Art, designed by Kamran Diba, was inaugurated in 1977 [@wp-tmoca]. The Iranian crown jewels, last used by the Pahlavi dynasty, are housed in the Treasury of National Jewels in Tehran; ownership of the imperial treasury was transferred to the state in 1937 [@wp-jewels]. ## Foreign policy

The shah supported regional co-operation, improved relations with the Soviet Union, and expanded Iran's armed forces; in 1972 Nixon allowed him to buy any conventional weapon in the US arsenal [@loc19]. Iran and Iraq signed the Algiers Agreement in 1975 [@loc19].

## Opposition and repression

After the 1963 riots there was little overt opposition, but new groups committed to armed struggle formed; the Fadayan attacked a gendarmerie post at Siahkal in February 1971, and 341 guerrilla members died between 1971 and 1979 in confrontations, executions or custody [@loc20]. In 1975 a one-party state was established under the Rastakhiz party, which the Library of Congress says further alienated the educated classes [@loc21]. Organisations such as Amnesty International drew attention to the treatment of political prisoners [@loc21].
