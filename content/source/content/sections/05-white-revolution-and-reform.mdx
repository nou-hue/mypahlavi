## After the coup

The United States arranged US$45 million of immediate economic assistance for the Zahedi government; diplomatic relations with Britain were restored in December 1953 and a new oil agreement followed in 1954 [@loc18]. Iran joined the Baghdad Pact in 1955 and signed a bilateral defence agreement with the United States in 1959 [@loc18]. Internally, the Library of Congress records a period of repression: the Tudeh, the National Front and other parties were banned or suppressed, the press muzzled, and the secret police SAVAK strengthened [@loc18].

## Plans and discontent

Rising oil revenue financed the Second Development Plan (1955–62), but the Library of Congress notes that inflation and discontent spread, and fraud charges forced the cancellation of the 1960 Majlis elections [@loc18]. In May 1961, yielding to domestic demands and to pressure from the Kennedy administration, the shah named Ali Amini prime minister [@loc18]. In January 1962 the Amini cabinet approved the land distribution law, which the Library of Congress calls the single most important measure of his fourteen-month government [@loc18].

## The 1963 referendum

In January 1963 the shah submitted six measures to a national referendum: land reform, profit-sharing for industrial workers, nationalisation of forests and pastureland, the sale of government factories to finance land reform, a change in the electoral law, and a Literacy Corps [@loc18]. He called the package the White Revolution, and the government announced a 99 per cent majority in favour [@loc18]. In February he announced that women would be given the vote [@loc18].

## Opposition

Many clerical leaders opposed land reform and women's suffrage and were concerned about the extension of royal authority [@loc18]. In June 1963 Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini was arrested in Qom after a speech attacking the shah; three days of violent riots followed and were severely suppressed [@loc18].

## The royal family

The shah remarried in 1959; his wife Farah Diba Pahlavi gave birth to a son, Reza, in 1960 [@loc19].
