## Army and administration

Crowned as Reza Shah Pahlavi in April 1926, the former officer built Iran's forces into a disciplined army of 40,000 and in 1926 persuaded the Majlis to approve universal military conscription [@loc15]. He used the army to bolster his own power and to pacify the country and bring the tribes under control; in 1924 he broke the power of Shaykh Khazal in Khuzestan, and he forcibly settled many tribes [@loc15]. The administration was overhauled and the bureaucracy greatly expanded [@loc15].

## Law and schooling

Many of the measures were designed to reduce the power of the religious hierarchy [@loc15]. Educational reform ended the clerics' near monopoly on schooling, and a codification of laws created a body of secular law applied by a secular judiciary; clerics were excluded from judgeships and the task of notarising documents passed to state-licensed notaries [@loc15]. The civil code, the work of Justice Minister Ali Akbar Davar, was enacted between 1927 and 1932 [@loc15]. In 1928 the capitulations, under which Europeans were tried in their own consular courts, were abolished [@loc15]. A system of secular primary and secondary schools was created and the country's first European-style university was established in Tehran [@loc15]. A law establishing the University of Tehran was introduced in the Majlis on 13 March 1934, and the university was inaugurated on 4 February 1935 [@iranica-higher-education]. Accounts that give 1934 for its inauguration appear to be dating the law rather than the opening.

## Dress and women

Reza Shah imposed European dress, opened schools to women and brought them into the work force; in 1936 he forcibly abolished the wearing of the veil [@loc15]. A decree of 8 January 1936 banned veils and was swiftly and forcefully implemented [@wp-reza].

## The railway

The Trans-Iranian Railway, a 1,394 km line between the Caspian Sea and the Persian Gulf, opened fully in 1938 [@unesco1585]. UNESCO inscribed it on the World Heritage List on 25 July 2021 [@unesco1585]. The Library of Congress also records that the shah expanded the road network and established state-owned factories for textiles, matches, canned goods, sugar and cigarettes [@loc15].

## The cost

The same sources record the other side of the ledger. The shah took effective power from the Majlis, muzzled the press and arrested opponents; several religious leaders were jailed or exiled [@loc15]. In 1936 troops entered the shrine of Imam Reza in Mashhad, where worshippers had gathered to protest the reforms; dozens were killed and many injured [@loc15]. Powerful tribal chiefs were put to death, and the court minister Abdul-Hosain Teimurtash was jailed and quietly executed [@loc15]. The shah amassed great tracts of land, tax policy weighed heavily on peasants, and by the mid-1930s there was considerable dissatisfaction [@loc15]. In foreign affairs he cancelled the Anglo-Persian Oil Company's agreement in 1932; a new and improved agreement was signed but did not satisfy Iran's demands, and on the eve of the Second World War Germany was Iran's largest trading partner [@loc15].
