## A constitution under pressure

Public anger at Muzaffar ad Din Shah's borrowing and his concessions to Europeans led merchants and clerical leaders to take sanctuary in mosques in January 1906, and some 10,000 people took sanctuary in the compound of the British legation in Tehran in June [@loc13]. In August the shah was forced to issue a decree promising a constitution; an elected assembly convened in October and drew up a text providing for an elected parliament, the Majlis, and a cabinet subject to its confirmation [@loc13]. The shah signed the constitution on 30 December 1906 and died five days later [@loc13]. The Supplementary Fundamental Laws of 1907 provided, within limits, for freedom of the press, speech and association [@loc13].

## Closure and restoration

The next shah, Mohammad Ali, set out to crush the constitution. In June 1908 he used the Russian-officered Persian Cossacks Brigade to bomb the Majlis building and close the assembly; resistance gathered in Tabriz, Esfahan, Rasht and elsewhere, and in July 1909 constitutional forces marched on Tehran, deposed him and re-established the constitution [@loc13]. In 1907 Britain and Russia had agreed to divide Iran into spheres of influence, which ended hopes of a new era of independence from the great powers [@loc13]. In December 1911 the Majlis refused a Russian ultimatum over the American treasurer-general Morgan Shuster; Bakhtiari chiefs then surrounded the Majlis, forced acceptance, and the constitution was suspended again [@loc13].

## War and a failed protectorate

Iran declared neutrality in the First World War but became a battleground for Russian, Turkish and British troops [@loc14]. After the war Britain was the dominant outside influence in Tehran; Lord Curzon's 1919 agreement, offering a loan and advisers, was widely viewed as establishing a British protectorate, and the Majlis refused to approve it [@loc14].

## Reza Khan

In February 1921 a Persian Cossacks Brigade officer, Reza Khan, in collaboration with the journalist Sayyid Zia ad Din Tabatabai, marched into Tehran and seized power [@loc14]. Reza Khan quickly became the dominant figure, minister of war and, in 1923, prime minister, when Ahmad Shah agreed to leave for Europe; the shah never returned [@loc15]. He considered founding a republic, as Atatürk had in Turkey, but abandoned the idea in the face of clerical opposition [@loc15]. In October 1925 a Majlis dominated by his supporters deposed the Qajar dynasty, and in December it conferred the crown on Reza Khan and his heirs [@loc15].

## Places that remain

The Golestan Palace complex in Tehran, which took its most characteristic form in the nineteenth century when the Qajar family chose it as their royal residence, was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List on 23 June 2013 [@unesco1422].
