## The invasion

Iran declared neutrality at the outbreak of the war, but when Germany invaded the Soviet Union in 1941 the Allies urgently needed to move war material across Iran, and Britain was already annoyed that Iran had refused to expel German nationals [@loc16]. Britain and the Soviet Union invaded simultaneously, the Soviets from the north-west and the British from Iraq and from the head of the Persian Gulf [@loc16]. Most sources date the start to 25 August 1941; the Library of Congress gives 26 August [@wp-anglosoviet; @loc16]. Resistance quickly collapsed [@loc16].

## Abdication

Reza Shah abdicated on 16 September in favour of his son, who became Mohammad Reza Shah Pahlavi [@loc16]. The British took Reza Shah and members of his family first to Mauritius and then to Johannesburg, where he died in July 1944 [@loc16].

## The supply route

Britain, the Soviet Union and the United States together moved over five million tons of munitions and other war material across Iran to the Soviet Union [@loc16]. In January 1942 Iran signed a tripartite treaty of alliance in which the Allies agreed to respect Iran's independence and territorial integrity and to withdraw within six months of the end of hostilities [@loc16]. In November 1943, at the Tehran Conference, Roosevelt, Churchill and Stalin reaffirmed that commitment [@loc16].

## Hardship and politics

The Library of Congress records that food and essential items were scarce, inflation severe, and that foreign troops accelerated social change and fed nationalist sentiment [@loc16]. With the controls of Reza Shah's reign gone, party and press activity revived; the communist Tudeh Party was especially active among industrial workers [@loc16].

## Azarbaijan and Mahabad

In December 1945 the Azarbaijan Democratic Party announced an autonomous republic, and activists in Kordestan established the Kurdish Republic of Mahabad; both were supported by Soviet forces still in the country [@loc16]. Soviet troops withdrew in May 1946, partly as a result of United States, British and UN pressure, and in December the Iranian army entered Azarbaijan, after which the Pishevari government collapsed; the Mahabad republic met a similar fate [@loc16]. In the new Majlis a National Front bloc led by Mohammad Mossadeq helped defeat a Soviet oil concession by 102 votes to 2 [@loc16].
